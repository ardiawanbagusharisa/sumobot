The sharpest point (Chinese closing, 01:07–01:11): you don't need a finished MVP to run a demand study. Only test users who have already taken action — already using or paying for a competing tool, or a teacher actively hunting for one. Someone who has taken no action has no budget and no real demand; they'll be polite and teach you nothing. Correct order: user → user journey → MVP scope → curriculum → infrastructure. The team is running it backwards. Teachers can also co-create the curriculum, which converts your stated weakness into a channel.

2. Summary

The pitch argues from features and top-down market percentages instead of evidence of demand, and has no identified first customer. The 20-person study was the most valuable asset in the room but was presented with no methodology, no participant profiles, and no stated learning — so it read as opinion. The team's own stated problems (curriculum, infrastructure) are premature: both are unanswerable until you know who the first user is. Target for November isn't a perfect pitch — it's a pitch backed by 1–2 real validation experiments and a defined beachhead.
